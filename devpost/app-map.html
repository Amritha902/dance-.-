What it is

Mimic teaches a dance from a video. It tracks the dancer's body, shows it as a cute mirrored character you can slow down and loop, scores you live on camera with limbs that light up yellow, orange or red when they're off, and an AI coach tells you which step to drill and how.

Browser: seeing

MediaPipe finds 33 body points per frame. Scoring, the character, steps and hookstep all run here. Camera frames never leave the device.

Reference route (three stops)

A route to read in your editor; it hasn't been walked through together yet.

  1. web/js/screens/battle.js, search for function draw(). Every frame it gets the dancer's pose at the video's current time and your latest camera pose, and calls compare(...). The line const limbColors = limbColorsFor(cmp); is where the colours come from, and judge.push(t, cmp, extra) records the moment for the score and the coach.
  2. web/js/pose/compare.js, search for export const SEVERITY and export function compare. Each limb becomes bone directions, so distance from the camera doesn't matter; its angle off decides the colour (yellow 25°+, orange 37°+, red 50°+).
  3. web/js/draw.js, search for const tint inside drawFigure. Each limb is painted with its override colour or the body colour, and the face switches to "oops" when something is wrong.

A practice to reuse

Test against data whose answer you already know. The hookstep finder was first checked on a synthetic routine built to repeat every 3 seconds (test/choreography.test.mjs, "the repeating phrase is found as the hookstep"). It answered 6 seconds, a real bug: longer repeats were judged against a wider baseline and looked more prominent. The fix is the comment "Each lag is judged against a fixed-width neighbourhood" in web/js/pose/timeline.js. On a real video the bug would have looked plausible; against a known answer it was obvious. The same move works on any project: before trusting a detector, scorer or parser on real data, feed it something constructed so you know what it must say.
